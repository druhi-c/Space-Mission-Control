Model equations

a = R + h  ·  R = 6,371 km v(circular) = √(μ / a)  ·  μ = 398600.4418 km³/s² T = 2π √(a³ / μ)  [seconds] v(vis-viva) = √(μ (2/r − 1/a))

Altitude is measured above the surface; radius is measured from Earth's centre. On a circular orbit they differ by exactly 6,371 km.

Elliptical motion is propagated by solving Kepler's equation (M = E − e·sin E), not by constant angular speed.

Method, validation & assumptions

  • Constants: gravitational parameter μ = 398600.4418 km³/s², mean Earth radius R = 6371 km. All internal arithmetic uses kilometres and seconds; period is stored in seconds and shown in minutes/hours; distances can be displayed in kilometres or miles (1 mi = 1.609344 km).
  • Circular orbit: a = R + h, v = √(μ/a), T = 2π√(a³/μ), eccentricity exactly 0. Altitude and radius are constant and differ by R.
  • Elliptical orbit: shape from a and e: perigee r_p = a(1 − e), apogee r_a = a(1 + e). Speed uses the vis-viva equation at the satellite's current radius — the circular formula is never applied at arbitrary positions. Position comes from Kepler's equation solved by Newton–Raphson, so the satellite speeds up at perigee and slows at apogee exactly as real orbital motion does.
  • No Earth intersection: eccentricity is capped at e_max = 1 − R/a, so perigee radius is always ≥ 6,371 km. If you raise eccentricity or lower altitude, the limit is applied automatically and explained in the parameters panel — the app never shows NaN, Infinity or an orbit through the Earth.
  • Geostationary reference uses h ≈ 35,786 km, circular, equatorial, prograde. Its model period is ≈ 86,141 s — within 0.03% of one sidereal day (86,164.1 s), so it reads as ≈ 23.93 h. The published 35,786 km figure is referenced to Earth's equatorial radius (6,378 km); with this model's mean radius (6,371 km) an exact sidereal-day period corresponds to ≈ 35,793 km. This is an idealization: a real geostationary orbit also experiences lunisolar perturbations, solar radiation pressure and J₂ oblateness, and requires station-keeping.
  • Model limits: ideal two-body dynamics only — no atmospheric drag, no Earth oblateness (J₂), no third-body perturbations (Sun/Moon), no thrust, no inclination (the orbit is drawn in the equatorial plane). These effects are deliberately ignored so the altitude–period–velocity–eccentricity relationships stay clear.
  • Time scaling: the animation is compressed — at ×1.0 the simulation advances 300 simulated seconds per real second (shown in the header). The MET readout is simulated elapsed time, not a real clock, and the motion shown is not real-time.
  • Page wiring: this file is orbital-lab.html; Solar System Explorer links to solar-system.html; Mission Planner links to mission-planner.html. All three pages are self-contained and cross-linked.
