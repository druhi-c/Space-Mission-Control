Data & method — real values vs. visual parameters

  • Real published values (used in the data, telemetry and comparison panels): mean radius, mean heliocentric distance, sidereal orbital period, sidereal rotation period, temperature and classification are standard reference figures (NASA planetary fact sheets / IAU conventions). Temperature context is stated for each body — surface for rocky planets, 1 bar cloud-top level for the giants, photosphere for the Sun.
  • Dated satellite counts: confirmed natural satellites as of mid-2026 (IAU Minor Planet Center–announced orbits): Mercury 0, Venus 0, Earth 1, Mars 2, Jupiter 115, Saturn 293, Uranus 29, Neptune 16. These counts rise as new discoveries are confirmed, so treat them as a dated snapshot.
  • Visual parameters (not real): orbital radii are logarithmically compressed (0.39–30.07 AU mapped onto the drawing) and orbits are drawn as circles — eccentricity and inclination are ignored. Planet diameters use a compressed power scale, so the giants are drawn far smaller than reality and Mercury far larger, purely for legibility.
  • Angular speeds are compressed, not equalised: each planet keeps its real period ordering via T′ = 365.26 d × (T / 365.26 d)^0.55, so Mercury really does lap Neptune many times over — inner planets are exaggerated, outer planets slowed so they remain visible. Nothing in this model implies equal orbital speeds.
  • The animation is illustrative. One Earth orbit takes 20 s at 1× speed, and the simulation clock advances 18.26 simulated days per real second at 1×, which is why the date readout races forward.
  • Page wiring: this file is solar-system.html; the nav links to orbital-lab.html (Orbital Dynamics Lab) and mission-planner.html (Mission Planner). All three pages are self-contained and cross-linked.
