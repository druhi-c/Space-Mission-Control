      How the ranking is calculated. Each profile is scored on four criteria, each mapped to a normalised 0–1 value so that kilograms, months, and dollars cannot distort the result. The four values are multiplied by the weights above (normalised to sum to 1) and added: score = wC·cost + wD·duration + wS·scientific + wR·risk cost = 1 − profile budget ÷ largest profile budget · duration = 1 − profile midpoint duration ÷ longest profile midpoint · scientific = 0.6 × profile capability index + 0.4 × payload utilisation · risk = 1 − complexity × tolerance factor (a low tolerance penalises complex profiles more). The ranking therefore reacts to both your priority weights and your payload, budget, and duration choices — a profile you cannot afford still ranks by its own merits, but is tagged infeasible for this config. Scores express relative fit only; they are not probabilities of success.

      What this model does and does not claim

      Established astronomical facts

      Orbital altitudes, planetary distances, orbital periods, and light-travel latency are physical values (rounded for display). They describe where each destination is, not what a mission to it would cost or how long it would take to build.

      General aerospace principles

      Delta-v grows with destination, transfers to distant bodies depend on launch windows, outer-planet missions need long-life power and autonomy, and payload fraction is limited by structure and propellant. These are qualitative rules of thumb, not performance models.

      Illustrative planning assumptions

      Every budget, duration range, payload capacity, mass range, and complexity index in this tool is a made-up planning figure chosen to make comparisons interesting. None is a quotation, a cost estimate, a schedule, or a guaranteed capability. Real missions vary by an order of magnitude or more.

      This is an educational decision-support simulator, not a professional mission-design tool. It does not model propulsion performance, trajectories, launch vehicles, thermal or power budgets, reliability, launch windows, or programme risk, and it cannot validate a spacecraft design or guarantee that a mission is feasible. Real feasibility depends on propulsion, trajectory, launch windows, orbital mechanics, spacecraft constraints, funding profiles, and many other factors this tool does not model.
